About

Background & interests

I enjoy working at the intersection of hardware and algorithms. My early training in mechanical system modeling and controls shaped how I think about physical constraints, while my later shift toward electrical engineering and machine learning showed me how to build robust computing systems on top of them.

At Michigan, I have been involved in tape-out-oriented VLSI design and GPU timing-error resilience research, including standard-cell layout, Razor-style error detection, and system-level evaluation. In parallel, I work on ML for medical imaging for head and neck cancer survival prediction using multi-modal CT/PET data.

Research

Selected projects & ongoing work

  • Timing-Error Resilient GPU with Razor III
    University of Michigan · VLSI & architecture · 2024 – present

    Working on standard-cell level design and layout of Razor-based flip-flops and integrating them into a GPU-like pipeline to study the trade-offs between aggressive voltage scaling, timing-error detection, and overall energy efficiency.

    Razor III TSMC PDK GPU pipeline
  • 3D-Printed Anisotropic Magnetic Coating for Capsule Robot
    UM Radiation Oncology & UT Austin · Robotics & fabrication · 2024 – present

    Designing a soft magnetic coating that enables directional rolling of a magnetically actuated capsule robot inside a complex environment, combining 3D printing, material characterization, and control-oriented modeling.

    Capsule robot Soft magnetics 3D printing
  • DeepMTS + Foundation Models for HNSCC Survival
    ML for Health · Medical imaging & survival analysis · 2024 – present

    Extending the DeepMTS framework with CT/PET foundation models to improve survival prediction for head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, focusing on multi-modal feature fusion and robust evaluation across cohorts.

    CT/PET Survival analysis Foundation models

    EECS 461 · Embedded control · 2024

    VLSI design flow · 2023 – 2024
